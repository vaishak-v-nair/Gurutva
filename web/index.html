Four questions, asked in order

01 / LICENSING

Could my assumptions ever have produced this measurement?

If not, nothing further is allowed to be claimed.

02 / CALIBRATION

When I say 95% sure, am I right 95% of the time?

Checked by simulation, against the sampling floor.

03 / STABILITY

Run it twice from scratch. Same answer?

At the real observation, not only in simulation.

04 / ADEQUACY

Can the answer reproduce the data it came from?

The gate that failed at the top of this page.

The first three ask whether the model is self-consistent. All three passed on a model that was wrong. Only the fourth asks whether it is right.

What it refuses to tell you

“Less than 106 Mt of CO2 escaped the storage complex.”
Computed, then refused. That bound is 1065% of everything injected, and it is set by the prior rather than the survey. An exclusion limit only means something for a body you can name.
“There is no leak outside the complex.”
Only above 72% saturation. A 400 m accumulation emptier than that cannot be ruled out by this survey at all, so no containment claim covers it.
“Rock density here varies by 0.021 g/cc.”
What the standard tuned regularizer implies if you read it as a statement about rock. Licensing failed it at the 100th percentile: that prior could not have produced the anomaly that was measured.

Every other tool in this business sells confidence. This one is built to withhold it.
